# Cybercrime in Gujarat — Threat Landscape, Cases, Enforcement, and Gaps

*Research brief for KANAD S.H.I.E.L.D (Ahmedabad City Police / Gujarat Police cybersecurity hackathon). Compiled August 2026 from open-source reporting. All figures are sourced inline; anything not independently corroborated is marked ⚠️ UNVERIFIED.*

---

## 1. The scale of the problem — and why the numbers don't agree with each other

Gujarat's cybercrime picture is reported through at least three incompatible counting systems, and understanding *why* they diverge is itself one of the state's core data problems.

**National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP / cybercrime.gov.in) — complaints, not FIRs.** Gujarat recorded **168,000 cybercrime complaints on NCRP in 2024**, the fourth-highest state after Maharashtra (~303,000), Uttar Pradesh (301,000) and Karnataka (169,000), just ahead of Delhi (153,000) [[IndiaSpend/iSignal dataviz](https://www.isignal.in/data-viz/dataviz-how-indias-cyber-crime-incidence-is-rising-972933)]. Nationally, NCRP incidents rose from 452,000 in 2021 to 2.27 million in 2024 — a fivefold jump — with 1.25 million more filed just in the first half of 2025 [same source].

For calendar 2024 specifically, Gujarat's own police data (cited in state recovery announcements) puts complaints at **1.31 lakh (131,000) between 1 January and 15 December 2024** [[DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2024/12/24/gujarat-police-recovers-returns-over-108-crore-to-cyber-fraud-victims-in-2024/)] — broadly consistent with the NCRP figure.

For the first nine months of **2025**, Gujarat Police data reported to the state legislature/press shows **142,476 individuals targeted, of whom 72,091 were actually defrauded, for a total loss of ₹678 crore** [[The420.in](https://the420.in/gujarat-cybercrime-losses-hit-678-crore-72000-victims-targeted/)]. District/city breakdown for that period: Ahmedabad City 28,382 complaints, Surat City 20,102, Vadodara City 10,433, Rajkot City 7,643, Gandhinagar 4,884, Bharuch 4,404, Ahmedabad Rural 4,367, Valsad 3,871, Bhavnagar 3,663, Mehsana 3,618, with the remaining ~25 districts accounting for the rest [same source].

**NCRB "Crime in India" — registered FIRs, a much smaller and *declining* number.** Under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita / IT Act categories that NCRB tracks as formally registered cyber crime cases, Gujarat shows **1,995 cases in 2023 falling to 1,592 in 2024 — a >20% decline** even as complaint volume rose [[Gujarat Samachar/NCRB](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/ahmedabad-cyber-crimes-surge-61-in-2024-amid-statewide-decline-ncrb-37168049478.html)]. This is the single most important structural fact in this brief: **only roughly 1 in 80–100 NCRP complaints in Gujarat becomes a registered FIR.** The gap is not necessarily fraud — many complaints are duplicate, low-value, or resolved by freezing without an FIR — but it means most "cybercrime in Gujarat" as experienced by citizens never shows up in the crime-statistics system a hackathon team might reach for first.

City-level NCRB detail for 2024: **Ahmedabad city cases rose 61%, from 246 (2023) to 396 (2024)** — of which 229 (57%) were cheating/forgery, 36 computer-related offences, 30 identity theft, 40 obscene/sexually explicit material; 343 arrests (327 male, 16 female) [same source]. **Surat city fell 32%, from 397 (2023) to 266 (2024)** [same source] — a genuine surprise given Surat's reputation (see §9) as a mule-account and SIM-smuggling hub; this likely reflects registration practice rather than actual crime volume, since Surat features constantly in state-level and CBI raids.

Nationally for context: NCRB recorded **86,420 cybercrime cases in 2023 rising 17.9% to 101,928 in 2024** (cyber-fraud rate up from 6.2 to 7.3 per lakh population); **72.6% of all 2024 cases (73,987) were financial fraud**, followed by sexual exploitation (3,190), extortion (2,536), causing disrepute (2,231), and personal revenge (1,850) [[Vajiram & Ravi, NCRB 2024 report](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/crime-in-india-2024/)].

**Financial losses, freezing, and recovery.** Gujarat Police's State Cyber Crime Cell **recovered/returned over ₹108 crore to victims in 2024**, having **frozen ₹285.12 crore over the year** — meaning less than 40% of frozen money was actually returned within the year [[DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2024/12/24/gujarat-police-recovers-returns-over-108-crore-to-cyber-fraud-victims-in-2024/)]. A special **Lok Adalat drive (3–13 December 2024)** processed 40,905 applications, with an additional ₹75 crore expected to be released to victims through that channel [same source]. Nationally, cumulative figures reported as of 28 February 2025 show **3.8 million fraud complaints citing ₹36,448 crore in losses, against only ₹60.52 crore recovered to users** [[iSignal/IndiaSpend](https://www.isignal.in/data-viz/dataviz-how-indias-cyber-crime-incidence-is-rising-972933)] — a recovery rate under 0.2% by this measure. ⚠️ This figure sits awkwardly next to a separately reported Deccan Herald headline citing "over ₹10,300 crore siphoned off by cyber criminals since 2021" per I4C — the two national numbers use different scopes/time windows and are not reconcilable from what was found; treat any single "amount lost nationally" figure with caution and always check the reporting window.

For elderly-targeted investment fraud specifically, one Gujarat cluster case shows the recovery math starkly: **₹35.34 crore defrauded from seniors in Ahmedabad, Vadodara, Valsad and Rajkot, with only ₹4.62 crore (13%) recovered** [[Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/cyber-fraud-of-35-crore-targets-seniors-in-gujarat-only-4-62-crore-recovered-27272193862.html)].

**The 1930 helpline and e-Zero FIR.** Gujarat rolled out a **"Cyber Financial Fraud e-Zero FIR"** service in 2026 letting victims register a Zero FIR purely by calling 1930, without visiting a police station, to compress the "golden hour" before stolen money is layered/withdrawn [[ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/national/general-news/gujarat-launches-cyber-financial-fraud-e-zero-fir-service-victims-can-register-complaints-from-home20260727123630/); [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-police-introduces-instant-e-zero-fir-for-cyber-fraud-cases-just-call-1930-96151945963)]. The **first e-Zero FIR under this system saved ₹15.76 lakh** for an Ahmedabad digital-arrest victim [same source].

## 3. Deepfake and AI-enabled crime — the visible data gap

This is the topic area with the thinnest Gujarat-specific evidence, and that thinness is itself a finding worth reporting to hackathon teams.

- **AI voice cloning**: no Gujarat-specific case with a named victim/amount was found. The nearest concrete Indian case located is **Lucknow's first AI-generated voice fraud**, where a cloned "distant uncle" voice extracted ₹44,500 from a 25-year-old [[Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/india-lucknow-reports-first-ai-generated-voice-fraud-case-1.1702870783452)]. Nationally, Haryana's cyber cell reported **2,300+ voice-cloning fraud cases in Q4 2025 alone — a 450% year-on-year increase** ⚠️ UNVERIFIED beyond the citing source [[Phobolytics](https://phobolytics.com/blog/voice-cloning-ai-india-deepfake-protection-2026)]. Given Gujarat's outsized digital-arrest caseload (which already uses fake "officer" video calls extensively — see §2), it is highly plausible voice-cloning is already embedded in some of those calls but is not being tagged separately in reporting.
- **Deepfake video / face-swap extortion**: Gujarat Police's own **Operation Surakshit Cyberspace** (July 2026) explicitly names **"sextortion, deepfakes and cyberstalking"** as one of its two campaign-week themes, run across 3,415 sessions in week two alone [[NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/gujarat-police-launches-operation-surakshit-cyberspace-build-safe-897.htm)] — meaning Gujarat Police is treating deepfakes as an active, current threat to citizens even though press coverage of specific prosecuted Gujarat deepfake cases is sparse in open sources.
- **AI-generated CSAM**: no Gujarat case found. India's legal apparatus (principally POCSO) is built around physical/tangible abuse material and has a documented gap in covering wholly-synthesised or face-swapped abusive imagery [[CCRNUS RLBlog](https://ccrnusrlblog.wordpress.com/2026/02/04/digital-darkness-indias-legal-vacuum-in-combating-ai-generated-child-sexual-abuse-material/)]. Globally, NCMEC reported **over 1 million CSAM reports tied to AI generation in nine months**, and IWF logged a **26,362% year-on-year increase** in such video reports — figures that make it near-certain India, and by extension Gujarat, is affected even without a locally reported case yet surfacing.

**Implication for the hackathon**: deepfake/voice-clone detection tooling is a track where Gujarat Police almost certainly has *anecdotal* internal awareness (hence naming it in public campaigns) well ahead of *documented* casework — a strong signal this is an area where a working detection/triage tool could have outsized value precisely because the institution hasn't yet built its own evidence base.

## 4. Enforcement architecture in Gujarat

- **State Cyber Crime Cell, CID Crime** — 7th Floor, C Wing, Block-2, Karmyogi Bhavan, Sector-10A, Gandhinagar; the apex state cyber investigation unit [[Gujarat Cyber Crime portal](https://gujaratcybercrime.org/eng/guide.html)].
- **Gujarat Cyber Centre of Excellence (CCoE)**, under CID Crime, appears to be the specialised unit executing the state's highest-profile busts: the Purohit cyber-slavery arrest, the ₹226-cr dark-web/crypto syndicate case, and Operation Mule Hunt 1.0/2.0 are all attributed to it [[The420.in](https://the420.in/gujarat-myanmar-cyber-slavery-network-chinese-links-exposed/); [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/07/06/368569/bharat/operation-mule-hunt-2-0-gujarat-police-bust-rs-802-cr-cyber-fraud-network-arrest-khaliq-hussain-afzal-two-others/)].
- **District/range Cyber Crime Police Stations** — confirmed to exist at least for Ahmedabad range (Shahibaug/Dafnala) and Gandhinagar range [[Cyber Crime PS Gandhinagar Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/cybercrimegnr/?hl=en)], with city cyber cells also operating in Surat, Vadodara and Rajkot (all four cited as jointly cracking 12 cases alongside the State Cell in a mid-May 2025 crackdown on a China/Dubai-linked gang [[DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2025/05/17/major-crackdown-against-cyber-crime-in-gujarat-accused-linked-to-china-dubai-based-gangs-held/)]).
- **Reporting channels**: dial **100 or 112** (for the seven newly formed districts) routes to a district **Incident Response Unit (IRU)**; dial **1930** for the national cyber helpline, which in Gujarat now auto-generates an **e-Zero FIR** (2026 rollout) [[Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-police-introduces-instant-e-zero-fir-for-cyber-fraud-cases-just-call-1930-96151945963)]. A **Cyber Crime Prevention Unit (CCPU)** handles threat analysis and citizen/police education.
- **AASHVAST** — launched by Union Home Minister Amit Shah on **10 January 2020** in Gandhinagar as **India's first Cyber Crime Prevention Unit**, providing a dedicated victim helpline and cyber-security guidance portal [[ForumIAS](https://forumias.com/blog/home-minister-launches-countrys-first-cyber-crime-prevention-unit-aashvast-in-gandhinagar/)]. Launched alongside it: **VISHWAS** (Video Integration and State Wide Advanced Security) for CCTV-based law-and-order/traffic/forensic video analytics, and **Netrang**, establishing command-and-control rooms across **33 districts** [[IAS EXAM](https://www.iasexam.com/home-minister-launches-aashvast-and-viswas-projects/)].
- **Cyber Commando training** — Gujarat's **Rashtriya Raksha University (RRU), Gandhinagar** is one of seven national institutions (with IIT Kanpur, IIIT Kottayam, IIT Madras, etc.) selected under the MHA/I4C plan to train **5,000 cyber commandos over five years**; the first batch of 30 officers (drawn from states including Gujarat, Chhattisgarh, and Andaman & Nicobar) completed the six-month residential course and graduated around March 2025, covering ethical hacking, digital forensics, threat intelligence, cloud/IoT security, malware analysis and cryptocurrency-crime skills [[Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/career-and-campus/top-news/2025/03/25/indias-first-cyber-commandos-graduate.html); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2060928)].
- **I4C coordination**: the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre under MHA is the national nodal body Gujarat's units plug into for 1930/NCRP, inter-state case linking (e.g., Mule Hunt cases spanning 21–22 states), and international cooperation, including a **2025 India–US MoU on cybercrime investigation** executed via I4C [[Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/india-us-sign-mou-on-cybercrime-investigations)].
- **KANAD S.H.I.E.L.D itself** is run by **i-Hub Gujarat in partnership with the Ahmedabad Cyber Crime Branch (Ahmedabad City Police)**, based at Bungalow No. 15, Nr. IPS Mess, Dafanala Cross Road, Shahibaug — i.e., the same Ahmedabad Cyber Crime Police Station address noted above — explicitly framed as a "Startup-Police Cybersecurity Innovation Program" with a pilot-deployment and government-procurement pathway for winning teams [[YouTube listing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_CXryX4Si5s&vl=en-IN); [FundsForNGOs listing](https://fundsforcompanies.fundsforngos.org/events/startup-police-cybersecurity-innovation-program-cybershield-ahmedabad-india/)]. Note: kanadshield.com itself returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch during this research; teams should browse it directly in a normal browser for the authoritative problem-statement list.

## 5. Conviction, pendency, and the accountability gap

This is arguably Gujarat's (and India's) least-visible but most structurally damaging weakness.

- **National conviction rate is near-zero relative to case volume.** Of roughly 1.67 lakh cases registered nationally 2020–2022 across all 28 states, only **2,706 persons were convicted — 1.6%** [[Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/only-1-6-conviction-rate-in-2-yrs-amid-surge-in-cybercrime-cases)]. A separate chargesheet-to-conviction breakdown shows the rate bouncing between 2.7% and 7.9% across 2017–2021 (2017: 152/5,180 = 2.9%; 2018: 490/7,000 = 7.0%; 2019: 360/9,000 = 4.0%; 2020: 1,109/14,087 = 7.9%; 2021: 490/18,000+ = 2.7%), with **18 states/UTs recording zero convictions in 2021** and Uttar Pradesh the best performer that year (292 convictions from 4,407 chargesheets) [[ThePrint](https://theprint.in/tech/why-are-cybercrime-convictions-low-in-india-weak-forensics-dark-net-cross-border-attacks/1273191/)].
- **Pendency is structurally worse in Gujarat than the national norm.** As of 2021, Gujarat was named alongside Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh as a large state where **cybercrime case pendency exceeded 90%** [[ThePrint](https://theprint.in/tech/why-are-cybercrime-convictions-low-in-india-weak-forensics-dark-net-cross-border-attacks/1273191/)]. Combined with the NCRB finding above that Gujarat's *registered* case count actually **fell** in 2024 even as *complaints* rose sharply, the honest read is: Gujarat's front door (1930/NCRP/e-Zero FIR) has scaled up dramatically, but the back end (investigation → chargesheet → conviction) has not kept pace, and may even be shrinking in registration terms.
- **Root causes cited by experts** (India-wide, applicable to Gujarat): weak digital-forensics capacity and training gaps in evidence handling; dark-web anonymity tools that are "readily available online"; cross-border/jurisdictional friction requiring slow international cooperation; platform privacy policies at Google/Meta/Twitter(X) that constrain how fast investigators can get subscriber data; absence of standardised inter-state investigation procedures. A proposed fix floated by experts is a **"hub-and-spoke" centralised knowledge/evidence database** to standardise procedure across states [[ThePrint](https://theprint.in/tech/why-are-cybercrime-convictions-low-in-india-weak-forensics-dark-net-cross-border-attacks/1273191/)].
- Gujarat's specific escalation pattern for its highest-value cases (e.g., the two ₹30-crore digital-arrest cases) is to **hand off to CBI** once state investigation identifies an interstate/international syndicate [[Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/retired-professionals-from-gujarat-duped-of-over-rs-30-crore-in-two-digital-arrest-scams/)] — itself an implicit admission that state-level cyber units, however well-resourced by Indian standards, hit a jurisdictional and capability ceiling on cross-border syndicate cases.

## 6. Platform cooperation and jurisdiction friction

- **Telegram** states it provides "expeditious cooperation" and runs a dedicated India channel monitored 24×7 [[ThePrint reporting cited via search](https://theprint.in/tech/telegram-meta-google-oppose-governments-proposal-for-kill-switch-to-curb-digital-arrest-scams-cite-unintended-risks/2850652/)]. **Meta** says it discloses subscriber info/IP logs/device details on valid legal request and runs training sessions with I4C.
- Despite this, **Telegram, Meta and Google jointly opposed a government-proposed "kill switch" mechanism to curb digital-arrest scams**, citing unintended risks [[ThePrint](https://theprint.in/tech/telegram-meta-google-oppose-governments-proposal-for-kill-switch-to-curb-digital-arrest-scams-cite-unintended-risks/2850652/)] — a live example of the enforcement-vs-platform tension that directly bears on Gujarat's digital-arrest caseload, since nearly every one of the state's marquee digital-arrest cases (§2) runs over WhatsApp/video-call infrastructure controlled by these same platforms.
- India's government has separately pushed back on **WhatsApp's and Telegram's planned "username" features**, worried that moving contact discovery away from phone numbers will make it harder to trace fraud-account operators to real identities [[TechPortal](https://thetechportal.com/2026/07/02/india-expands-probe-into-username-features-sends-notices-to-telegram-and-signal-report/)] — directly relevant to Gujarat's mule-account and SIM-fraud investigations (§2), which currently anchor much of their tracing capability on phone-number-linked KYC.
- The **Supreme Court designated CBI as the nodal agency for digital-arrest cases** and directed intermediaries to cooperate on tracing coordinated networks [search-derived, multiple sources above] — consistent with Gujarat's practice of escalating its biggest digital-arrest cases to CBI.
- **India–US MoU on cybercrime cooperation**, executed through I4C, is the clearest bilateral channel for chasing the US-facing fake-call-centre operations that keep surfacing in Ahmedabad/Junagadh (§2) [[Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/india-us-sign-mou-on-cybercrime-investigations)].

## 7. The Southeast Asia "cyber-slavery" pipeline as a distinctly Gujarat-linked crisis

This deserves separate emphasis because it inverts the usual victim/perpetrator framing: Gujarati youth are simultaneously **victims of human trafficking** and (once trapped) **forced labour inside the fraud machine that then victimises other Indians and foreigners**.

- The state's own **CCoE arrested the alleged kingpin, Nilesh (Neel) Narpatsinh Purohit**, describing his network as "one of the largest human-trafficking and cyber-fraud networks ever uncovered by an Indian agency" [[The420.in](https://the420.in/gujarat-myanmar-cyber-slavery-network-chinese-links-exposed/)].
- Recruitment specifically targeted Gujarati youth with **₹70,000/month Thailand data-entry job offers**; once in Bangkok, victims were smuggled across the **Moei River into Myanmar** and held in **armed, walled compounds**.
- Financial structure: **$2,000–4,500 per recruit** to the top agent, up to **40% shared down to 126 sub-agents**; victims then treated as "debtors" owing **₹3–4 lakh** to be released.
- IPS officer **Rajdeepsinh Zala** (quoted): Purohit "monitored Interpol's wanted list to check if his name appeared before planning his next move" [[The420.in](https://the420.in/gujarat-myanmar-cyber-slavery-network-chinese-links-exposed/)] — illustrating the operational sophistication and evasion-awareness of these networks.
- National scale: **~6,998 Indians rescued from Southeast Asian scam compounds since 2022** (2,533 from Cambodia, 2,297 from Laos, 2,168 from Myanmar), with a further ~4,000 rescued/repatriated through coordinated India-Thailand-Myanmar efforts over the last three years [[The420.in](https://the420.in/southeast-asia-cyber-slavery-human-trafficking-scam-compounds-indians/)].
- This pipeline is mechanically fused to Gujarat's SIM-fraud problem (§2): the same Ahmedabad-origin SIM-smuggling operations feeding Cambodia/Myanmar (Rawal/Paradiya/Thakkar; Lakod) are plausibly provisioning the phone infrastructure these compounds use to call Indian victims — a supply chain a hackathon tool could target at the SIM-activation/KYC layer rather than only at the call-detection layer.

## Unsolved problems a hackathon could attack

1. **Mule-account onboarding friction, not just detection.** Every major Gujarat case (Mule Hunt 1.0/2.0, ₹1,052 cr combined across the two operations) shows fraud rings recruiting *real* individuals to open *real* accounts at *real* banks using legitimate-looking paperwork (fake firm registration, rented KYC). Post-hoc detection (flagging accounts after ₹800 cr has already moved) is late. A tool that scores account-opening applications for mule-recruitment patterns (e.g., an account opened by someone who fits a "recruited via Telegram commission" profile: young, first-time account, immediately linked to a shell-firm name like "Chamunda Communication") at the point of opening — not after — directly targets the financial-plumbing weak point officers themselves named as the effective lever (Operation Mule Hunt's own framing: "targeting the financial infrastructure... rather than merely arresting individual fraudsters").
2. **SIM-to-scam-compound traceability.** The same Ahmedabad-origin actors (Rawal/Paradiya/Thakkar; Lakod) keep surfacing smuggling bulk SIMs to Cambodia/Myanmar/Dubai — the literal telecom backbone of both the digital-arrest scam wave and the cyber-slavery compounds. A tool correlating telecom KYC-activation bursts (e.g., one person activating dozens of SIMs in a short window) with outbound courier/travel patterns could interdict this supply chain upstream of any individual fraud call.
3. **Digital-arrest golden-hour compression.** Gujarat's two ₹30-crore CBI-escalated cases ran 82 and 123 days respectively before discovery — meaning existing bank/telecom fraud-alerting entirely failed to flag sustained, unusual outflows from elderly, previously low-activity accounts over months. A behavioural-anomaly alerting layer for banks (flagging a retiree's account making its first-ever large RTGS transfers in a multi-week isolation pattern) sits squarely inside the "prevent the transfer before day 80" problem officers have not solved.
4. **NCRP-to-FIR conversion transparency.** The unexplained gap between 142,476–168,000 annual NCRP complaints and only ~1,600–2,000 registered FIRs is a trust and workflow problem as much as a technical one. A case-triage/prioritisation dashboard that helps a resource-constrained Cyber Crime Cell decide which of ~150,000 complaints/year merit FIR registration and investigation — based on recoverability window, amount, syndicate linkage, and repeat-offender signatures already visible in prior mule-account data — could measurably close this gap.
5. **Deepfake/voice-clone detection for the specific "officer on video call" digital-arrest format.** Since Gujarat's dominant fraud typology already relies on fake uniformed "officers" on video calls, and Gujarat Police has pre-emptively flagged deepfakes as a citizen-facing risk, a lightweight, real-time or post-hoc detector tuned specifically to the "fake CBI/police officer video call" pattern (rather than generic deepfake detection) is a narrowly scoped, evidence-backed track.
