## 1. Top-Level Overview

- **Formed:** May 1, 1960, when Gujarat separated from the former Bombay State. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))
- **Headquarters:** Police Bhavan, Sector-18, Gandhinagar (state capital), with a major presence also in Ahmedabad. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))
- **Jurisdiction:** ~196,024 km², population 60.4 million (2011 census; higher today). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))
- **Controlling ministry:** Gujarat Home Department, under the Chief Minister's office structurally but run day-to-day by the Home Minister.
- **2025–26 Police budget:** ~₹12,659 crore (~US$1.3 billion). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police)) — ⚠️ UNVERIFIED against a primary Finance Department budget document; multiple search attempts to reach the official line-item budget book failed (site blocked / not found). Treat as a secondary-source figure.
- **Motto framing used in official communication:** "Seva, Suraksha, Shanti" (Service, Security, Peace). ([gujaratcybercrime.org](https://gujaratcybercrime.org/eng/vikash_sahay.html))

## 4. Rank Structure (top to bottom)

Director General of Police (DGP) → Additional DGP (ADGP) → Inspector General (IG) → Deputy Inspector General (DIG) → Superintendent of Police (SP) / Commissioner of Police (city) → Additional SP / Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP) → Assistant SP / Assistant Commissioner of Police (ACP) → Deputy SP (DySP) → Police Inspector (PI) → Sub-Inspector (PSI) → Assistant Sub-Inspector (ASI) → Head Constable → Police Constable / Lok Rakshak. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))

## 5. Territorial Structure: Commissionerates, Ranges, Districts

### 5.1 Commissionerates (city police, headed by CP, IPS)

Currently **4 Commissionerates**: **Ahmedabad, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot.** ([Wikipedia via search synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))

A **5th — Gandhinagar Commissionerate** — has been approved in principle and is described in media as "pending for three years," with implementation blocked on central government approval for salary/technical grants. It would absorb the current Gandhinagar SP jurisdiction (Gandhinagar city + Kalol, Mansa, Dehgam talukas) plus parts of Kadi. ([Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-police-set-for-major-reshuffle-new-ranges-and-commissionerates-on-the-cards))

**Ahmedabad City Police** structure (as the largest/model commissionerate):
- City divided into 2 sectors → currently **7 DCP zones**; an **8th zone** is proposed to cover the fast-growing western corridor (Bopal, Sola, Sarkhej, Bodakdev) to reduce load on existing zones and speed response times. ([Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-police-set-for-major-reshuffle-new-ranges-and-commissionerates-on-the-cards))
- Functional branches: Crime Branch, Control Room, Traffic Branch, Police HQ, Special Branch, Administration. ([Wikipedia via search synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_City_Police))
- 14 traffic police stations in Ahmedabad city. ([search synthesis, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_City_Police))

### 5.2 Ranges (headed by IG/DIG)

**As of the last stable count: 9 ranges** — Ahmedabad, Vadodara, Gandhinagar, Surat, Rajkot, Junagadh, Bhavnagar, Dahod-Panchmahal, and Border Range. ([multiple sources synthesized, incl. Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))

**Major 2025 change — Border Range split in two:**
- Gujarat split its international-border policing into **two ranges**, creating a **10th range**. The original **Border Range** (created May 20, 1995, from Rajkot Range; covers 406.87 km land + 238 km marine = 645 km of the Pakistan border) is now paired with a new **Banaskantha Range**, headquartered at **Palanpur** under a separate IG-rank officer, covering **Banaskantha, Patan, and the new Vav-Tharad district**. Rajkot Range retains the upper-Saurashtra districts. ([EaseMyPrep](https://easemyprep.in/news/1854-gujarat-divides-pakistan-border-policing-into-two-ranges-what-changes-and-why))

**Further restructuring proposed (not yet implemented as of the reshuffle reports):**
- A new **Kutch–Morbi Range** combining East Kutch, West Kutch and Morbi SPs (aligned to the Kutch Lok Sabha constituency).
- A new **Mehsana Range** grouping Patan, Banaskantha, Vav-Tharad, and Mehsana.
- These would take total Range-IGP posts from 9 to **10**, and would reorganize the **34 SPs** currently spread across the 9 ranges. About 70% of Kutch police personnel currently hail from Banaskantha/Patan, complicating the redraw. ([Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-police-set-for-major-reshuffle-new-ranges-and-commissionerates-on-the-cards))

⚠️ Conflicting sources: some pages describe the Banaskantha Range as already the "10th range" (implemented), while the Gujarat Samachar reshuffle piece describes further Kutch-Morbi/Mehsana ranges as still "on the cards." Treat the exact current range count (9 vs 10 vs a future 11) as fluid and verify against the latest Gujarat Police / Home Department notification closer to the hackathon.

### 5.3 Districts / District Police (headed by SP)

- Gujarat has **33 police districts** (police administration) plus the **Western Railway Police** as a distinct unit. ([search synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Police))
- **Vav-Tharad became Gujarat's 34th administrative district**, carved out of Banaskantha, officially notified Sept 25, 2025 and effective **October 2, 2025**. It comprises 8 talukas (Deodar, Lakhani, Tharad, Rah, Vav, Dharnidhar, Suigam, Bhabhar) and ~600 villages, bordering Rajasthan and the Rann of Kutch. ([DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2025/09/25/gujarat-govt-notifies-creation-of-vav-tharad-district-new-talukas/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vav-Tharad_district), [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-gets-34th-district-with-tharad-carved-out-of-banaskantha))
- ⚠️ It is unclear from available sources whether police-district reorganization has caught up with the new administrative district count (33 → 34), i.e., whether Vav-Tharad now has its own SP office or is still policed as part of Banaskantha. Flagged as a gap.

### 5.5 August 2025 Mega-Reshuffle (context for institutional churn)

On **August 18–19, 2025**, the Gujarat government transferred **105 IPS/SPS officers** (74 IPS + 31 SPS per one tally) in one of the largest reshuffles in recent years, timed just ahead of Ganeshotsav/Navratri to bolster festival law-and-order arrangements. Distribution: **Gandhinagar 20, Ahmedabad 19, Surat 13, Vadodara 11, Rajkot 7**, plus South Gujarat (8), Central-North Gujarat (13), and Saurashtra-Kutch (10) districts. Moves touched district SPs, cybercrime/EOW leadership, prison administration, and railway/coastal security posts. Manoj Agarwal became DGP of CID (Crime & Railways) in this round (later succeeded by Dr. K.L.N. Rao). ([DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2025/08/19/gujarat-govt-transfers-105-ips-sps-officers/), [Indian Bureaucracy](https://www.indianbureaucracy.com/gujarat-govt-transfers-105-senior-police-officers-district-wise-details-inside/), [The Secretariat](https://thesecretariat.in/article/gujarat-govt-issues-transfer-orders-for-105-ips-state-police-service-officers))

## 7. Manpower: Sanctioned vs. Actual Strength, Vacancies, Recruitment

### 7.1 Headline numbers (⚠️ figures are from a **January 2022** data point — the most granular figures found; treat as dated but directionally still relevant)

- **Sanctioned strength:** 174.39 police personnel per lakh population (2022); this had fallen from **198 per lakh nationally in 2018** as a benchmark comparison.
- **Actual strength:** 127.82 per lakh population (2022).
- Historical trend: 2018 — sanctioned 168.9/lakh, actual 120.2/lakh, total sanctioned strength 84,476; 2020 — sanctioned 162.72/lakh, actual 122.78/lakh.
- **Total shortfall: 25,000+ officers** relative to sanctioned strength, per Gujarat Samachar's read of the data.
- Using 2011-census population (6.03 crore) as the baseline, Gujarat "should" have **1,04,400 police personnel** but had only **76,200 (73.08%)** — a gap of **~28,200**.
- ([Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-falls-short-of-over-25-000-cops-to-meet-centres-reform-guidelines))

### 7.2 National comparison (BPRD / India Justice Report data, 2023–2025)

- **National average (Jan 2023):** 1 police officer for every **831** people.
- **Sanctioned national PPR:** 195.39 per lakh (down from 198 in 2018); UN-recommended benchmark is **over 220 per lakh**.
- **Gujarat is one of 7 states/UTs** (with Assam, Bihar, Dadra & Nagar Haveli–Daman & Diu, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Rajasthan, West Bengal) where **one police officer serves over 1,000 people** — i.e., Gujarat is **below** the national average on this metric.
- National vacancy: **5.31 lakh police posts vacant** across all states + **1.27 lakh vacant** in Central Armed Police Forces.
- Women in police (national): **10% of force strength** vs **48%** of population; up from earlier years (~doubled since 2014). Gujarat-specific women% not found — gap.
- Gujarat **does meet its OBC/SC/ST reservation quotas** at the officer level in the police force — one of only 9 states to hit the OBC officer-level target, and one of only 2 states (with Karnataka) to meet SC/ST/OBC quotas in **both** police and judiciary.
- ([Drishti IAS / BPRD](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-news-analysis/data-on-police-organisations-bprd), [The Print / India Justice Report](https://theprint.in/india/1-cop-for-every-831-people-is-national-average-but-in-bihar-its-1-cop-for-1522-india-justice-report/2590794/))

### 7.3 IPS-specific officer shortage (very hackathon-relevant — leadership bandwidth problem)

- Gujarat's **sanctioned IPS cadre is 208 posts**; as of **January 2025**, only **194 were filled** — a **14-officer direct vacancy**.
- **24 Gujarat-cadre IPS officers are on central deputation** (the state is entitled to send up to 45 under central policy, but this is already straining state postings).
- **14 officers were holding "additional charge"** of a second post on top of their own, at the time of reporting.
- Context cited: **rising cybercrime** and other offenses are outpacing capacity; many senior officers were pulled to IB/CBI; the situation was flagged as likely to worsen with the **June 2025 retirements** of DGP Vikas Sahay and senior officer Vivek Srivastava.
- ([The Secretariat](https://thesecretariat.in/article/gujarat-ips-shortage-24-on-central-deputation-14-with-additional-charge))

### 7.4 Recruitment drives (2024–2026)

- **2024 drive:** 12,472 vacancies notified.
- **2025–26 drive (current, GPRB — Gujarat Police Recruitment Board):** **13,591 Class-3 vacancies** — **12,733 Lokrakshak (Constable/LRD)** + **858 PSI (Sub-Inspector)**. Category split: UR 5,436; OBC 3,669; SC 2,038; ST 1,019; EWS 1,429. Applications opened **Dec 3, 2025**, closed **Dec 23, 2025**; written (Lokrakshak) exam initially set for **June 15, 2025** and a subsequent sitting held **June 14, 2026** (multiple/staggered exam dates across the cycle — dates appear inconsistent across sources, ⚠️ verify exact exam calendar).
- Separately, the Home Department earlier announced plans to recruit **14,820 police personnel + 245 civil staff by 2025** — likely the parent target that the 2024 and 2025–26 drives are drawing down against.
- ([MaruGujarat](https://www.marugujarat.in/2025/11/gujarat-police-recruitment-2025/), [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/gujrat-police-recruitment-2024-notification-out-for-12472-vacancies-eligibility-selection-process/amp), [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/gujarat-police-to-recruit-14-820-personnel-by-2025))

### 7.5 Recruitment-exam integrity problems (highly relevant — a clear "trust/process" pain point a hackathon team could target)

- Gujarat has had **13 documented government-recruitment paper leaks in 9 years (2014–2023)**, including at least one **directly involving police recruitment**: the **2018 Lok Rakshak Dal (LRD)** exam was among that year's leaked papers (alongside Mukhya Sevika/policewomen-adjacent posts, TAT teacher recruitment, Nayaab Chitnis revenue posts).
- Other notable leaks: 2014 GPSC officer exam; 2015/2016 Talati (clerk) exams; 2019 non-secretariat clerk exam (cancelled Dec 2019); 2021 head clerk exam (paper obtained from a printing-press manager, sold to candidates, 6 arrested, retest ordered); 2021 DGVCL/electricity-board exams; 2022 forest guard exam; **Jan 2023 GPSSB junior clerk exam** — the biggest, affecting **9.5 lakh aspirants**, exam cancelled after a raid by the Gujarat **ATS** on a Vadodara computer centre at 1:30 am; **19 people ultimately arrested**.
- **Government response:** the **Gujarat Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Bill, 2023** — minimum 3 years' imprisonment + ₹1 lakh fine, up to 10 years + ₹1 crore fine; 7–10 years + ₹1 crore minimum for organized-crime-conspiracy-level leaks; convicted candidates barred from state exams for 2+ years; all offenses non-bailable, non-compoundable, cognizable; investigated by an Inspector-rank-or-higher officer.
- ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/states/west/gujarat/after-13-govt-exam-paper-leaks-in-nine-years-gujarat-proposes-new-law), [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/guj-exam-for-recruitment-govt-posts-cancelled-after-paper-leak-key-accused-held-gujarat-head-clerk-scam/amp), [Wikipedia list of paper leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_paper_leaks_in_India))

**Hackathon angle:** the direct link between a **2018 LRD (constable) paper leak** and today's still-unfilled 12,733 LRD vacancies is a strong narrative — tech solutions for tamper-proof, leak-resistant recruitment/exam-delivery pipelines would land well with this audience.

## 10. Structural Pain Points Reported in Media

1. **Top-leadership churn:** Three different DGPs in roughly 18 months (Sahay → Rao → Malik, Dec 2025–June 2026), with the final choice described by observers as "surprising" — suggests instability/politicization risk at the very top even as the state pushes major tech modernization.
2. **IPS officer shortage:** 14 vacant sanctioned IPS posts, 24 officers away on central deputation, 14 more juggling dual "additional charge" postings — thinning senior supervisory bandwidth exactly as cybercrime and organized crime cases are rising.
3. **Rank-and-file shortfall:** ~25,000–28,000 person gap between sanctioned and actual strength (on 2022 figures); Gujarat sits among only 7 Indian states/UTs where a single officer serves over 1,000 residents, worse than the national 1:831 average.
4. **Recruitment-integrity crisis:** 13 paper leaks in 9 years including a direct LRD (constable) leak in 2018 — undermines public trust in the very pipeline meant to close the manpower gap; a new 2023 law criminalizes this more heavily, but the underlying process/technology risk (how exams are set, printed, transported, digitally distributed) remains a target-rich area.
5. **Marine/coastal infrastructure decay:** Of 31 patrol boats for the state's 30 Marine Police Stations along 1,600 km of coastline, **7–8 are non-functional at any given time** — a hard readiness gap on a coastline that also sees India's largest heroin/cocaine seizures (Mundra port, Kutch coast).
6. **Overwork / occupational stress / mental health:** Academic studies (Vadodara-focused) found **58.33% of police personnel report moderate occupational stress**, 35% high, 6.67% low; broader Indian reporting links "long hours, staff shortages and repeated exposure to trauma" to burnout, anxiety, and elevated suicide risk among police nationally (Gujarat-specific suicide statistics were **not found — gap**, though the underlying stressor literature is Gujarat-focused).
7. **Reorganization backlog:** The Gandhinagar Commissionerate upgrade has been "pending for three years" awaiting central government sign-off on salary/technical grants — a bureaucratic bottleneck rather than a design problem, but it means five of Gujarat's biggest urban/administrative centers (Ahmedabad, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot, and the pending Gandhinagar) still don't have uniform command structures.
8. **Range boundaries lag population/security reality:** the Border Range split (2025) and proposed Kutch-Morbi / Mehsana ranges show the state visibly playing catch-up on territorial reorganization — including a live tension where ~70% of Kutch's police personnel are natively from Banaskantha/Patan, complicating any clean jurisdictional redraw.
9. **New cybercrime unit is still being stood up:** as of Aug 2025 announcement, the dedicated statewide Cyber Crime Unit (5 SPs/8 DySPs/15 PIs) was newly approved but its building, financing vehicle, and full staffing were still pending — meaning cybercrime response capacity is currently transitional/under-resourced relative to case growth (₹892 crore single fraud network busted in Sept 2025 alone).
10. **ACB enforcement gap in leadership continuity:** ACB went **4 years without a dedicated full-time Director** before Piyush Patel's Feb 2025 appointment — a specific example of the same "who's actually in charge" churn problem seen at the DGP level, just one rank environment down.
